# Lexicon

In-universe slang, patois, and terminology established in play.

## General Forge Slang

**Jin**: A lucky charm or personal token.  From djinn (Arabic), worn smooth over centuries of use until the religious meaning faded and only "lucky thing you carry" remains.  See also: [[Characters/Leight/Black Iron Totem]].

**Gilt**:  Rich people.  The gold-plated class.  Used dismissively by everyone who isn't.  "No way gilt would pay to come here."

**Drift**:  Salvage.  Flotsam.  Stuff found adrift, tossed "ashore," or otherwise without clear provenance.  Like driftwood washing up.  Can refer to scrap, junk, found objects, or people operating at that social stratum.  "He's drift" = he's nobody, scrap-tier.

**Drift shop**:  A junk store, salvage market, or second-hand parts dealer.  Sells whatever washed up.  Ranges from organized storefronts to a tarp on the deck with a pile of scrap.

**Ox scrubbers**:  Slang for oxygen/CO2 scrubbers aboard ships and stations.  Nobody says "oxygen reclamation system" unless they're writing a manual.

**Caf**:  Caffeine drink.  Generic term covering everything from proper brewed coffee-analogues to synthetic caff pills dissolved in hot water.

**Cafshop**:  Any small establishment serving caf.  Not fancy.  Counter service, maybe a few stools.

**The well**:  Planetside, relative to an orbital station.  "Going down the well" = taking the shuttle to the surface.  "Up the well" = back to orbit.

## Paxton / Marigny Local

**Dip the shrine**:  Touch or acknowledge a small shrine/memorial in passing, for luck.  A habitual gesture, like crossing yourself.  Leight does this passing through R5.

## Celadon / Orris

**Solar**:  Produce grown under a real star in actual soil.  The Forge equivalent of "organic."  Premium product; station-siders pay absurd prices for food that didn't come from a hydroponics bay.  "You can taste the difference."  Can also describe the growing method: "solar garden," "solar-grown."

**Pip**:  One-eighth of a pod.  The cubical structural frame that is the base manufacturing unit of the [[Lore/Equipment/Forge Modular Container System|Forge Modular Container System]].  From "PIP" (Pearson Industrial Platform), stamped on every frame.  Nobody consistently remembers what the acronym stands for.  Mostly relevant to mechanics and builders; everyday people think in slabs and peas, not pips.

**Pea**:  A standard shipping container.  Two pips joined into a rectangle (2×1×1) with double doors on one end.  The universal unit of cargo logistics.  What gets dropped, hauled, loaded into cargo holds.  From "Pearson" worn down to a syllable.  Four peas to a pod.  On stations where space is at a premium, a pea might also be entry-level housing (tight, but livable for a single occupant).

**Slab**:  A 2×2×1 footprint (four pips).  Standard habitation unit at [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/The Racks|Celadon]]: three pips of interior space plus one pip as a roofed balcony/porch.  Also used for cargo when a wider footprint is needed.  Whether a slab is "standard" housing or aspirational depends entirely on where you are; planetside where containers are surplus, slabs are default.  On a station where volume is expensive, a slab is comfortable living.  Two slabs stacked = one pod.

**Mod**:  One module.  A 4×4×4 pip cube (eight pods).  The standard structural unit of space station architecture.  Rings are built from mods.  From "module" but nobody says the full word.  Scale: pip (room) → pod (house) → mod (building/city block).

**Corvin** (also "hex clad"):  Generic term for the stamped polyalloy panels bolted onto PIP frames to create enclosed containers.  Originally the Corvin® HexClad™ Polyalloy Panelling System; now genericized the way "kleenex" ate the tissue category.  Knockoffs everywhere, all functionally identical (except *that one brand* that doesn't interlock properly).  Mechanics say "hex clad" or "hex panel"; everyone else says "corvin" or just "panel."

**The watchtower's still standing**:  Morbid greeting among perimeter crews.  Meaning: nothing's changed, we're still here, the jungle hasn't swallowed it yet.  Refers to the unfinished vista tower visible above the canopy.  The day you can't see it anymore, things have gotten worse.

**The 'racks**:  Worker housing at Celadon Terminus.  Stacked repurposed supply containers, three high.  From "barracks" but nobody says the full word.

**The wire**:  The perimeter edge.  "Past the wire" = into forgespawn territory.  There is no actual wire; it's a gradient of cut ground, scorched ground, and regrowth.  The term is borrowed from military usage and stuck.
