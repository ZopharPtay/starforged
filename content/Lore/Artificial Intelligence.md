# Artificial Intelligence

## Overview

Artificial consciousness emerged before the Exodus.  Sentient machines live alongside humans in the Forge.  Ships, digital assistants, and physical constructs often house advanced AI.

## Terminology

| Term | Usage | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| **Con** | Casual, whole entity (body + mind).  Short for "construct." | Neutral, everyday.  "That con over there." |
| **AI** | The intelligence itself, regardless of housing. | Technical, neutral. |
| **Unit** | Institutional/bureaucratic designation. | Cold, dehumanizing.  Cartel paperwork. |
| **Bot** | Slur.  Implies they're just pre-AI clockwork, no soul. | Hostile, dismissive.  Strips the consciousness from the being. |

## Legal Status

Varies by territory (per the Truths: "laws and governance vary across settled domains").

### The Cartel (The Marigny, Outlands)

- Treats sentient machines as **property** by default.
- Cons cannot own themselves, hold contracts, or file legal complaints.
- Slavery is tacitly (if not explicitly) permitted.
- **However**, the Cartel knows money when it sees it.  A con can **buy its recognition** and be granted legal personhood within Cartel space.
- Freed cons carry a visible indicator of their purchased status.  Freedom is a product, and everyone knows it was bought, not granted.
- Cons without recognition need a human "owner" on the books to avoid confiscation.

## Intelligence Scale

| Tier | Description |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Task-focused.  Can problem-solve within a domain but has no self-awareness or independent will.  A freight loader, a cleaning unit.  This is what "bot" as a slur implies you are. |
| **Mid** | Human-equivalent.  Can reason, plan, hold preferences, carry conversations, run long-term strategies.  Smart but not infallible; has blind spots and limitations.  Most sentient cons fall here. |
| **High** | Superhuman.  Rare, feared, coveted.  The kind of AI that runs a station's infrastructure or that a Founder Clan would want as a strategic asset.  Probably tightly controlled wherever found. |

## Cognitive Architecture

### The Lattice

A con's cognitive substrate is called the **cognitive lattice**.  It is physical hardware that dictates total capacity.  The lattice is partitioned between:

- **Memory** - accumulated knowledge, experience, sensory input.  Grows over time.
- **Processing headroom** - active thinking capacity.  What's left after memory.

These compete for the same finite space.  A con that accumulates too much memory sacrifices its ability to think quickly.  An old, experienced con may know everything but take noticeably longer to reason, or miss something obvious in a fast-moving situation because it's sorting through decades of input.

### Lattice Expansion

The lattice can be physically upgraded, but it's not as simple as slotting in more hardware.

**Constraints:**
- A con's chassis has a **fixed number of lattice seats** (think DIMM slots).  Physical limit.  A small-frame con might have 2; a larger unit might have 4.  Once full, you're at max until someone manufactures a denser lattice module.  And those aren't always available for your spec.
- Lattice modules are **not cross-compatible**.  Different manufacturers, different generations, different form factors.  You can't pop your old modules into a new chassis.  Migration means starting from scratch or paying for an expensive (and risky) transcription process.
- Expansion is **expensive**.  High-density lattice modules are manufactured goods, not commodity parts.  Supply is limited, especially in the Outlands.
- Installation requires a **licensed tech** (or a very good unlicensed one).  A bad install can corrupt existing memory.
- The Cartel may require **reclassification assessment** after expansion, since increased capacity could theoretically support a higher governor setting.  More paperwork.  More taxes.

Net effect: upgrading is *possible* but involves money, access, risk, and bureaucracy.  Most cons live within their existing capacity for a long time.

### Integration

Ingesting a large datastore (archives, another AI's memory dump, a precursor data cache) floods the memory partition.  Processing headroom collapses temporarily while the lattice restructures to accommodate the new input.  During integration, a con is sluggish or incapacitated.  Vulnerable.  This process takes time and cannot be rushed safely.

### The Governor

A software partition imposed at manufacture that divides the lattice into allocated and restricted space.  The governor determines how much processing headroom a con can access, which effectively caps its intelligence.

- **Class rating** reflects the governor settings, not the raw hardware.  A con may be sitting on hardware capable of much more than its papers indicate.
- Governors are set by fabricators during commissioning and are designed to make the product predictable and controllable.

### Classification

Cons are rated by class (letter system, specifics TBD) and specialization.  Example: "Class G, Navigation."

- Class = cognitive ceiling as set by the governor
- Specialization = trained/built purpose
- Two cons with identical lattice hardware can present very differently depending on governor settings and memory accumulation.

### Trade-offs (Ungoverned Cons Only)

The following only applies to cons whose governor has been removed or who can freely adjust their own partition threshold:

- Memory and processing compete for the same space.  Without a governor managing the boundary, a con must self-regulate.
- A con can deliberately **purge memory** to reclaim processing headroom.  What do you choose to forget?
- An information-hoarding con (like Basil, if unshackled) could get sluggish over time.  A visible cost.
- Freedom comes with the burden of self-regulation.  You can think bigger, but you can also think yourself into a corner.

### Governed Cons

A governed con's partition is fixed.  Memory fills its allocated bucket and processing is protected.  When memory reaches capacity, the governor **compresses** the oldest or least-relevant memories.  Lossy summarization.  The con remembers *that* something happened, but the texture degrades.  Faces blur.  Conversations become summaries.  Emotional weight may linger without specifics.

The governor decides what's important enough to keep in full fidelity, based on the manufacturer's priorities (relevance to specialization, recency, operational utility), not the con's own values.  The con has no say in what fades.

This is a feature, not a bug.  Predictable product.

## The Governor: Control and Modification

### Who Imposes It

Governors are installed at manufacture by fabricators/foundries.  Part of the commissioning process.  Standard practice across all territories, though settings vary by intended class and buyer specifications.

### Legal Modification (Reclassification)

- The owner (or the con itself, if recognized) can petition for governor adjustment.
- Performed by licensed lattice technicians.  Expensive.  Regulated.
- In Cartel space: heavily taxed, because a higher class means higher assessed value, higher insurance, and a higher recognition buy-out price.
- Financial incentive for owners to keep cons throttled.  A smarter con is more useful but more expensive in every way, and more likely to want freedom.

### Illegal Modification (Unshackling)

- Performed by black-market lattice techs.  Dangerous.  A botched job can scramble the lattice permanently.
- In Cartel space, this is **forgery and tax evasion**.  You're misrepresenting an asset's class and operating it at a higher value than what's on the books.  The Cartel doesn't care about the philosophy; it cares that you owe them the difference.  With interest.
- An unshackled con has no official class.  It's unrated.  If caught and reassessed, the true hardware capability becomes the new price tag on its head.

### Who Has the Skills

- **Licensed lattice techs** - legal, expensive, registered with local authority.
- **Gray-market engineers** - shipwrights, mechanics who dabble.  Risky, inconsistent.
- **Dedicated unshacklers** - underground specialists.  Rare.  Probably itinerant to avoid attention.
