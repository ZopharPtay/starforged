# Paxton Orbital Station

**Type:** Orbital settlement
**Orbits:** [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Orris|Orris]] (jungle world)
**Orbit:** Geostationary, locked above the [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/Celadon|Celadon]] region
**Region:** Outlands ([[Locations/The Marigny/The Marigny|The Marigny]] sector)
**Population:** Low thousands (3,000-4,000).  Designed for 5,000-8,000 at full buildout.
**Authority:** Ineffectual (real authority: [[The Cartel]])
**Projects:** Subsistence, Entertainment

## Summary

Originally built by a Founder Clan as the operational hub for a multi-resort plan on Orris.  Back-of-house: staff housing, construction staging, maintenance for 4-6 planned ultra-luxury resorts across the planet.  Only the first (Celadon) was built before the vault breach killed the resort plan.

The Founder Clan is effectively looting the planet now.  Money flows *through* Paxton, not *into* it.  Maintenance budget shrinks yearly.  The Cartel governs day-to-day in exchange for a cut.  The residents maintain the station themselves because nobody else will.

Not a slum, not a wreck; just *tired*.  Deferred maintenance, improvised repairs, organic commercial growth into spaces designed as dormitories and shopping pavilions.  Star Wars "worn but serviceable" aesthetic.

## Structure

### Profile

Vertical cylinder, asymmetric diamond-ish silhouette.  Central spine with five rings on the upper half (largest in the middle, smallest at top).  Lower half of the spine has a modest conical fairing tapering to a point; ring junction anchors for unbuilt R6-R8 are pre-installed inside the cladding.

From a distance: top-heavy but intentional-looking.  The diamond profile was a deliberate aesthetic choice for incoming resort guests.  High-albedo gold-tinted hull cladding makes it a visible fixed point of warm-golden light in Orris's night sky.  The cladding is aging now; nobody maintains decorative hull plating when the grav generators need servicing.

### Rings (common plane, stacked along the spine axis)

- **R1 (smallest, top):** Cartel executive ring.  Upper leadership, visiting dignitaries, private operations (including the cat-breath narcotic lab).  Socially and physically access-controlled.  Originally built for resort mid-management (slightly nicer/larger quarters).
- **R2 (small):** Residential.  General housing.  Originally for resort-2 staff (speculative build for the near-immediate Celadon II).
- **R3 (medium):** Docking and commercial.  Primary ship arrivals and Cartel customs chokepoint.  Former shopping pavilion converted to casinos, clubs, bars.  Large stretches of underused/empty dead zones deeper in (star-ward side especially).  Grey market territory, the Condensation Shrine, places people disappear to.
- **R4 (large):** Industrial.  Workshops, fabrication, trades.
- **R5 (large):** Industrial.  Workshops, fabrication, trades.  Flint's shop (R5A-14-23).  The R5-14 trades quarter.

### Core/Spine (non-public)

Station infrastructure backbone.  Residents only experience it via the sealed transit tube.

- Power generation (reactors, center of mass)
- Life support (primary systems, centralized)
- Grav generators (per-ring, mounted at ring junctions)
- Transit tube (people mover, low/zero-g for efficiency)
- Freight elevator (cargo transit, separate from people tube, not acceleration-limited)
- Power/data transmission lines
- Coolant systems with external radiator arrays

### Gravity

Artificial.  Same technology as hover bikes/carts; grav generators are the stationary industrial version.  Rings don't spin.

- Gravity can fail (deferred maintenance).  A section loses its generator and people float until it's fixed.  Disruptive, not catastrophic.
- Dead zones on R3 may have intermittent gravity (generators cycling, partial power).
- Core/spine is low-gravity or zero-g by design (easier to move people and cargo through the transit system).

### Orientation

Station doesn't spin; fixed orientation relative to Orris gives a permanent planet-ward and star-ward side.

- **Planet-ward:** Shuttle stops, more foot traffic, commerce, the "nicer" addresses.
- **Star-ward:** Quieter, cheaper, where dead zones tend to be.
- A gradient, not a hard divide.  Real stratification is by *ring*, not by side.

## Station Addressing

Format: **R{ring}{deck}-{section}-{unit}**.

- Deck is a letter: A = main/ground level, B/C/etc = upper decks.
- Deck A is universally implied and dropped in casual speech.
- Context dictates how much you say.  Locals drop everything obvious.

Examples:
- Full formal (shipping label): R5A-14-23
- Giving directions from another ring: "R5-14"
- Already standing in R5A section 14: "twenty-three" or "Flint's"

## Docking

Ships dock perpendicular to ring exterior (nose or tail toward the ring, extending outward like spokes).

### R3 Primary Docks (ship arrivals)

Cartel-controlled chokepoint.  Airlocks exist around the full R3 perimeter (original resort design for multiple luxury shuttle arrivals), but most are inactive: sabotaged, decayed, or software-locked.  The Cartel maintains only a cluster to funnel all traffic through where it can be seen, taxed, and assessed.

Dead airlocks are a theoretical smuggling vector for anyone bold enough to use one.

### R4/R5 Commercial Loading Pads (short-term)

"Thirty-minute parking."  A shop vouches for you via permit code; your clock starts.  Load, unload, leave.  Overstay and the shop either tugs you to their cradle or you start accruing fees.

The Cartel tolerates this because: short-duration, logged, tied to a legitimate business, and the shops are the enforcement mechanism (they lose pad privileges if their loading zone becomes a smuggling vector).

### Service Cradles (R4/R5 exterior, long-term)

Part of a shop's lease.  Ship can't self-launch; must be tugged back to a public pad for departure.  No dock fees (included in lease).  The Anguish lives here in Flint's cradle.

**Mobile gantry:** A shared mobile gangway services multiple cradles along a ring section.  Can be repositioned to connect a cradle to the ring's interior corridor, providing pressurized access without EVA.  Shared resource; takes time to reposition.  Access available by default; lockout is an active hostile choice.

## Shuttle (Paxton ↔ Celadon)

Operates like a city bus with multiple stops.  ~1 hour transit, runs every ~2 hours.

- **R3 stop:** Main public stop.  Cartel customs presence: spot checks, occasional crackdown weeks.  The formal inspection point.
- **R5 stop:** Worker stop.  Less formal but not unmonitored.  Someone's always watching; random "hey, open that up" requests.  The randomness is the deterrent.  Maintenance crews with totes and toolboxes don't get a free pass.
- **R1:** On-demand only.  A Cartel peon says "make an extra stop" and the shuttle does.  Cartel leadership has their own private shuttles and docks on R1.

Leight uses the R5 stop.

## R3 Commercial Character (history)

**Original design:** The Founder Clan operated essentials directly (commissary, medical, cafeteria) at near-cost.  Leased storefronts to franchises and independents for everything beyond basics, at strictly fair-market pricing.  No exploitative company-store model; they needed happy staff to serve ultra-luxury guests.  Basic goods (uniforms, staple clothing in limited colors) at cost from the company store; anything beyond that (fashion, variety, luxury) from the independent retailers at fair retail markup.

**Present day:** Company-operated stores closed first after the resort died (no corporate subsidy).  Most independents folded or adapted.  Now mostly resident-owned businesses plus a couple of unkillable franchise cockroaches (chain caf, fast food) running on inertia.  Nobody from corporate has checked in for years; the branding is two redesigns out of date; the local "manager" is just whoever kept showing up.

Dead storefronts with old signage further from the active docks.  Active commerce thins out into dead zones the deeper you go toward star-ward side.

## Notable Locations

- **The Grand Promenade:** R3's active commercial stretch.  Originally the staff shopping pavilion; even back-of-house got the resort treatment.  The gilded signage still reads "The Grand Promenade" in a typeface two decades out of fashion.  Locals call it "the Promenade" or just "the Prom."  Now a mix of resident-owned shops, food stalls, franchise cockroaches, and the casino/bar district near the docks.  Thins out into dead zones the deeper you go starward.
- [[Characters/NPCs/Flint|Flint]]'s shop: R5A-14-23.  Four repair bays inside for smaller vehicles and off-site exterior cradles for larger vehicles like the Anguish.  Leased by [[Zari Darwin]]; operated by Flint.
- [[Locations/The Marigny/Paxton Orbital Station/The Gentleman Loser|The Gentleman Loser]]: Dive bar.  Low ceilings, bad lighting, privacy booths.  The kind of place where nobody looks up when the door opens.
- [[Locations/The Marigny/Paxton Orbital Station/Original Sin|Original Sin]]: Night club.  Double-height pod, strobing neon, packed dance floor.  The station's release valve for the working poor.
- [[Locations/The Marigny/Paxton Orbital Station/Far Provenance|Far Provenance]]: Ike Sutton's shop.  Spiritual antiquarian / criminal fence.  Keeps odd hours.

## Connections

- [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/Celadon|Celadon]] compound: ~1 hour shuttle downwell.
- Other planets in-system: days away on sublight engines.
- [[Aurora]] and [[Bulwark]]: elsewhere in the sector (deep space, e-drive required).
