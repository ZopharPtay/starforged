# The 'Racks

**Type:** Worker housing
**Settlement:** [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/Celadon|Celadon]]
**Construction:** Stacked [[Lore/Equipment/Forge Modular Container System|slabs]], three high

## Summary

From "barracks" but nobody says the full word.  Favela energy, not barracks grid; emergent architecture from standardized blocks.  The 'racks are what happens when you give a few hundred resourceful, tired, rained-on people a pile of universal building blocks and no architect.

## Construction

Each hab unit is a standard slab (2×2×1, four pips) with one pip's panels removed to create the balcony, giving an L-shaped interior (three pips of enclosed space) with a roofed, floored outdoor porch (one pip, open-air).  The structural frame (corner posts, roof plate, floor plate) remains intact regardless of which panels are removed; stacking integrity is unaffected.

Units are stacked three high in columns.  The orientation of each unit (which corner is punched) is a field decision made at placement.  Over time, residents and crews have discovered optimal arrangements:

- **Same-face punch:** Creates a column with aligned balconies.  Each porch has the unit above as its roof.
- **Rotated 90°:** Creates light wells between columns and cross-ventilation.
- **Inward-facing punch (adjacent columns):** Creates shared courtyard spaces between buildings.

Nobody designed this.  It emerged from years of workers stacking cans and figuring out what worked.

## Vertical Access

Bare-pip skeletons (unclad frames) assembled as scaffold stairwells.  Shared infrastructure; one stairwell serves a cluster of columns.  Switchback stairs inside the open frame, with landings at each level that connect to the horizontal bridges and gangways.  Not every column gets its own; you climb the nearest stairwell and walk along a bridge to reach your unit.

These are the most "official" structures in the 'racks; they have to bear traffic from everyone in the cluster, so they tend to be better-maintained than the improvised bridges.  Still bare pip skeleton, no cladding; open to the weather on all sides.  Rain runs down the stairs.  Nobody lingers.

## Bridges and Walkways

Connections between columns range across a spectrum of permanence:

- **Official gangways:** Metal grating bolted to structural posts.  Stenciled number.  Load-rated.  Nobody thinks about them.
- **Neighbor bridges:** Two planks, maybe three.  Rope railing on one side (the drop side; the wall side, why bother).  Been there so long the planks are stained dark.  A shared potted plant sits in the middle.
- **Commute shortcuts:** Single plank or repurposed shelving unit laid flat.  No railing.  Used by the person who built it and two others.  Will either get upgraded in six months or fall down in three.
- **Absent bridges:** Bolt holes and wear marks where something used to be.  Someone left, or fell, or maintenance pulled it.  People climb across anyway.

## Tenure and Class Indicators

- **Single-panel walls (new arrivals):** You hear everything.  Rain on hex-clad right next to your head.  Temperature swings with day/night.  Humidity condenses on interior surfaces.  The hex pattern is visible and tactile from inside.  You're sleeping in a shipping container and it never lets you forget.
- **Double-clad (long-timers):** Interior lining (scavenged hull plate, smooth-rolled sheet, whatever's available) with an air gap.  Deadens sound, buffers temperature, eliminates condensation.  Wiring and personal storage hidden in the gap.  The inside looks like a *room* rather than a container.
- **Proximity to the Cascade:** Correlates with tenure.  Close = early arrival, plumbed, established.  Far = newer construction, fewer amenities, longer walk to showers.

Over years, some long-timers have combined adjacent units into U-shaped suites with a shared courtyard.  Someone strings a tarp across the courtyard and it becomes a covered atrium.  A scrap-metal table gets bolted to the floor.  A micro-neighborhood forms vertically.

## Visual Texture

The 'racks aren't a uniform grid from outside.  Staggered rotation, mixed-brand panels (diamond-press repair patches on chevron-press originals), varied states of sealant decay, and improvised additions create a dense organic cluster.  Tarps directing rain toward collection barrels.  Cable runs for power strung between columns.  Boot racks on every exterior surface.  Cooking smoke drifting up through the gaps.

Older columns (near the Cascade) are bare alloy with blue oxidation dust.  Newer columns (compound edge) still have sealant sheen and legible manufacturer logos.

## The Shift Rhythm

The 'racks aren't uniformly alive or dead at any hour.  A night-shift column is silent at midday while the day-shift column next door has people cooking on their porches.  The bridges between those neighborhoods carry different traffic at different hours.

## Water

Each column or individual unit has its own rain catch.  See [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/Celadon#Water Infrastructure|settlement water infrastructure]] for details.

## In-Unit Amenities

- **Cooking:** Electric hotplates only (fire discipline indoors; open flame is fine on the porch).
- **Washing:** Ambient-temperature sponge bath from personal catch.  Good enough to rinse off before bed.  Not satisfying.  For a real hot shower, walk to the Cascade.
- **The porch:** Roofed, floored, open on two sides.  Hang a tarp or scrap-metal screen for semi-privacy.  In a jungle climate with constant rain, covered outdoor space is worth more than indoor square footage.  Hammocks, boot racks, cooking setups, social seating.
