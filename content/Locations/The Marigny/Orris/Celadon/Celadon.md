# Celadon

**Type:** Extraction compound / former luxury resort site
**Planet:** [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Orris|Orris]]
**Region:** Outlands ([[Locations/The Marigny/The Marigny|The Marigny]] sector)
**Population:** Hundreds (extraction workers, defense crews, mechanical support, admin)

## Summary

The sole human foothold on Orris.  Originally a luxury resort site; now an extraction compound maintained at enormous cost against relentless jungle and forgespawn pressure.  Built in a shallow crater bowl of dead soil from the original thermobaric site prep.

The settlement sits in a rough hub-and-spoke layout inherited from its military-camp origins: communal infrastructure central, housing radiating outward, workshops at the periphery, perimeter beyond.

## Structures

- **[[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/Celadon Terminus|Celadon Terminus]]:** The original resort welcome center.  Now the port of entry and operations hub.
- **[[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/The Plaza|The Plaza]]:** Paved terrazzo between the Terminus and the compound.  Terraced crater bowl with cascading drainage.
- **[[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/The Cascade|The Cascade]]:** Mess hall, communal showers, toilets.  The original infrastructure node the settlement calcified around.
- **[[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/The Racks|The 'Racks]]:** Worker housing.  Stacked slabs, three high, favela energy.
- **[[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/Rowenas Bay|Rowena's Bay]]:** Primary mechanical/vehicle maintenance facility.
- **Salvage yard:** South end, last thing before the wire.  Run by [[Characters/NPCs/Marrow|Marrow]].  Decommissioned shuttle wing on log posts; tarps radiating outward; log floor.
- **Spin launcher:** Centrifugal cargo launch system.  The drum is partially buried for stability; the exit tube angles outward and up past the perimeter.
- **Drop zone:** Where supply containers land.  Retro-burn scorches the area; workers rush to unload before regrowth.
- **Processing/staging:** Vault material prep before orbital launch.
- **Support services:** Medical, a bar or two, small market.
- **The perimeter:** Not a wall.  A gradient maintained by labor.  Cut ground, scorched ground, yesterday's regrowth, untouched canopy; all within meters of each other.

## Settlement Origin

The compound follows a military/remote-logging-camp layout that calcified in place.  The original structures changed; the relationships between them never did.

**Day zero:** GP-Medium tents forming shared mess/ablutions.  Workers in tent rows nearby.  Water collection, food prep, and sanitation as the first infrastructure; shelter secondary.

**The pattern:** Mess/showers central.  Housing radiating outward.  Workshops at periphery.  Perimeter beyond.  Hub-and-spoke inherited from that first camp and never disrupted because nobody had a reason to reorganize what already worked.

**The calcification:** Tents became peas.  Peas became slabs.  Slabs got stacked for density.  The mess tent became the Cascade.  The tool shed became Rowena's bay.  Marrow's yard grew at the edge where dead equipment accumulated.  The structures evolved; the layout fossilized.

Anyone with military experience (Marrow, Vanguard crews) clocks the geometry immediately: standard field camp with better materials.

## Defense Rhythm

- **Daytime:** Machete crews on the perimeter.  Maintenance cutting.  Spin launcher cycles.  Expedition prep.
- **Nighttime:** Flamethrower shifts.  The vine surge accelerates after dark.  The perimeter glows with a ring of fire.  Night is never truly dark.  You go to sleep smelling smoke.
- **Supply drops:** Targeted where growth is worst.  Retro-burn clears the zone; the clock starts immediately.

## Logistics

- **Up (spin launcher):** Centrifugal launch system.  Cargo pods are spun to orbital velocity in an enclosed drum and released on a tangential trajectory.  The sonic boom as a pod exits the launch tube thumps through the compound every few hours; you feel it in your chest.  Unmanned.
- **Down (retro-rocket drops):** Supply containers ([[Lore/Equipment/Forge Modular Container System|peas]]) with retro-rockets that fire before landing, incinerating the immediate zone.  Workers rush to unload before regrowth.  Containers get repurposed as infrastructure (housing, storage, perimeter material).  Nothing wasted.
- **Off-target drops:** They happen.  A near-miss (a few hundred meters past the perimeter) can be recovered: a small party with machete escorts, a plasma cutter, a barge to haul the contents back.  Non-trivial; requires the same group protocols as a hunting party.  They aren't salvaging the container itself, just the cargo.  Deeper misses are write-offs.  The deep canopy is littered with lost containers slowly being consumed.  Salvage hook for the brave or desperate.
- **Personnel shuttle:** Paxton ↔ Celadon.  ~1 hour transit, every ~2 hours.  For bodies only.
- **Executive visits:** Better shuttles exist.  The Director flies in, inspects, complains about yield, flies back up same day.  Never stays overnight.

## Cartel Presence

[[The Cartel]] is logistics: Teamsters, not owners.  They keep the shuttle running, workers in line, supply chain flowing.  They take their cut of everything that moves.  Dead-drop lockers in the terminal serve their communication network.

On top of the legitimate logistics, they run every traditional organized crime racket that makes money, legal or not.

## Water Infrastructure

### Principle: Decentralized

No centralized plumbing grid.  Each structure is responsible for its own water collection and processing.  No single point of failure, no political questions about who gets connected, no maintenance authority controlling access.

### The Cascade (communal)

Large roof surface, purpose-built collection and purification system maintained as shared infrastructure.  Supplies the mess kitchen, communal hot showers, and toilets.  This is the "official" water.

### The 'Racks (per-unit/per-column)

Each column or individual unit has its own rain catch.  Ranges from tarp-angled-into-barrel to proper gutter systems feeding a filter jug.  Some columns have pooled resources for a shared cistern at the top; others are every-unit-for-itself.

Supply is trivial on Orris (it rains constantly).  Filtration quality is the real variable.  The Cascade has proper purification.  A 'racks long-timer has a decent filter.  The new arrival is drinking rainwater through a cloth.

### Outbuildings (self-service)

Rowena's bay, Marrow's yard, and any other structure catch and process their own.  Generally trivial given the rainfall, but the infrastructure (cistern, filter, distribution) must be built and maintained by whoever occupies the space.

## Food Economy

### Hierarchy

- **Bottom tier (daily standard):** Ration packs.  Arrive via supply drop.  The Cascade kitchen prepares them in rotating variety.  It's fine.  It's always fine.
- **Middle tier (irregular):** Fresh game from hunting parties.  Shared among crews, traded for favors.  Shows up at Rowena's rooftop grill or the Cascade as tonight's special.  "I'll take your dawn shift if you save me a cut."
- **Top tier (luxury):** Solar produce.  Grown in actual soil under actual sunlight.  Private rooftop garden plots, jealously tended.  Theft from someone's garden is a genuine social offense.

### Hunting

Normal (non-forgespawn) jungle fauna exists alongside the cats and vines.  Hunting parties operate for recreation and protein supplement.  Groups only; the environment (cats, shock vines) makes solo excursions suicidal regardless of what you're hunting.

These legitimate parties provide the exact pattern the Cartel's narcotic crews hide inside.  Can't ban hunting without cutting off morale and food supplement.  "Had to kill a cat in self-defense" is always plausible because it's sometimes true.

### Solar Produce and the Upwell Economy

"Solar" is the Forge equivalent of "organic."  Food grown under a real star in actual soil instead of a UV lamp and hydroponics.  You can taste the difference.  Or people believe you can, which amounts to the same thing.

A tomato-equivalent grown in Orris dirt is worth absurd prices on Paxton.  Station-siders who've never been down the well pay for the novelty and status.  This inverts the expected class dynamic: the people doing the hardest work have access to something the station gilt can't get without going through them.

The Cascade's small kitchen garden provides occasional fresh additions to the mess line.  Private rooftop plots are personal income streams that never appear on any official ledger.

## Fire Culture

No fire fear outdoors.  The jungle is soaking wet.  Flamethrower crews burn the perimeter nightly.  The ambient humidity kills sparks before they land.  The compound permanently smells like smoke; adding a cookfire changes nothing.

Fire discipline exists only *inside* individual hab cans: bedding, clothing, personal belongings burn.  Electric hotplates inside; open flame outside.

Open grills, cookfires on the rooftops, warming drums near the perimeter; all unremarkable.  Fire is a tool here, a comfort, a constant presence.  Not a threat.

## The Vibe

- Everyone's too tired or too wet to care about strangers.  Nobody looks up.
- Working-class solidarity among the long-timers.  Outsiders aren't interesting unless they cause problems.
- Constant background sounds: machetes during the day, the crackle of fire at night, the spin launcher thump on schedule, the falls underneath everything.
- The perimeter crews have a look.  "Sometimes you're cutting vines and something you didn't see takes the person next to you."
- At night, the ring of fire on the perimeter means it's never dark.  You sleep surrounded by firelight and the smell of smoke.
