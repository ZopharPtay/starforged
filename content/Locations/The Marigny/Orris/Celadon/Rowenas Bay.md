# Rowena's Bay

**Type:** Mechanical / vehicle maintenance facility
**Settlement:** [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/Celadon|Celadon]]
**Operator:** [[Characters/NPCs/Rowena|Rowena]]
**Serves:** [[Factions/Vanguard Recon Group|Vanguard]] expedition crews, compound vehicle operations

## Summary

Primary maintenance facility for [[Lore/Equipment/Expedition Hover Barge|expedition barges]] and compound vehicles.  Her name is stenciled on the door.  Maintains vehicles for Vanguard expedition crews and compound operations.  When something's beyond repair, Rowena writes it off to [[Characters/NPCs/Marrow|Marrow]]'s salvage yard.

## Construction Method

Not standard slab-stack housing.  [[Lore/Equipment/Forge Modular Container System|PIPs]] used as *structural building blocks*: stacked as thick walls with exterior hex-clad panels and interior faces left bare.  The open frame cubbies become tool storage, parts bins, compressor housing, utility runs.  Each mechanic "owns" a section of wall; their tools, their parts, their mess.

A roof spans the full width between pip-stack walls, creating a column-free interior tall enough for a hover barge plus a person standing on top with a tool.

## Profile and Layout

U-shaped from the front: taller outer bays flanking shorter central bay(s).

- **Old bays (interior):** Lower ceiling, tighter.  Original construction.  Now service smaller equipment: personal vehicles, sleds, utility carts.  The oldest cubes; most accumulated grime and personality.  A mechanic's name stenciled on a frame post who hasn't worked here in five years.
- **New bays (outer):** Taller stacks, wider span, different roof pitch.  Built when larger expedition vehicles arrived.  Newer cubes, more organized.
- **The seam:** Where old meets new is visible.  Mismatched roof heights, an awkward gutter line, a walkway that steps up where floor levels don't match.  Long-timers navigate it without thinking; newcomers hesitate.

## Vertical Layering (within walls)

- **Ground floor cubes:** Open-faced workshop stations into the bay.  Welding bay (scorch marks on frame).  Electrical bay (tangle of test leads on hooks).  Paint area (if there is one; might just be "the corner where Rowena yells at you if you spray without a tarp up").
- **Second level:** Parts storage, compressor housing, breakroom cube (hotplate, someone's hammock).
- **Upper cubes:** Offices (Rowena's overlooks the bay floor through a cut window), bulk warehousing, records.
- **Roof:** Water infrastructure access (the original reason rooftop access exists).

## Bay Doors

Each door is a row of vertical ribbons, each one pip wide.  A ribbon is just standard hex-clad panels linked end-to-end vertically (as many as the bay is tall) and hung from a roller spindle at the top.  Roll it up; the chain of panels coils around the spindle.  Roll it down; full-height strip.  Line up as many ribbons side by side as the bay is wide.  All stock parts, no cutting, no custom orders.  Scalable to any dimension in pip increments.

- **Large bays (active service):** Ribbons are mostly rolled up during working hours.  Rolled down only to block sideways rain during heavy weather or when a job needs a cleaner environment.  No locking bar; these aren't secured overnight.
- **Small bays (overnight/secure storage):** Same ribbon system, but closed every night with a metal bar threaded through loops at the bottom of each strip.  Not high-security; "you can't just walk in."  Closing shop means rolling each ribbon down one at a time (thunk, thunk, thunk) and threading the bar.

When closed, the doors have a slatted appearance with thin vertical gaps between ribbons.  Light and sound leak through; you can tell if someone's working inside without opening up.  When open, the rolled-up ribbons sit in a row along the ceiling like a line of scrolls.

The roller mechanism is just a spindle; any mechanic can fabricate one.  Entirely field-built from materials on hand.

## The Rooftop

The lower central bay roof creates a sheltered terrace between the taller outer walls.  Wind-blocked, semi-private.

### Water Processing/Cistern

Takes up a significant chunk of the roof.  Feeds the bay's operational needs (coolant mixing, parts washing, cleanup) and the crew's private supply.  The original reason rooftop access exists; the social use grew around it.

### Garden

Tended by the least likely crew member (the catcalling "hey baby" type who goes quiet and careful when pruning).  Small but productive.  Herbs, greens, maybe a fruiting plant.  Solar produce that occasionally makes its way to the Cascade kitchen or upwell as barter.  Nobody gives him shit about it because the crew benefits.

### Social Area

Grew organically from two people on milk crates sharing a smoke to a proper crew space maintained with pride:

- Fabric canopy strung between the taller walls.  Not waterproof; just shade and "good enough" rain break.
- Open grill (no fire fear outdoors; the jungle is soaking wet and the perimeter burns nightly).  The surrounding roof plate is discolored from years of heat and grease.
- Seating evolved over time: hull plate across crates, a proper chair from somewhere, a frame member at exactly lean-height.
- Something competitive; target throwing, arm wrestling, running tallies scratched into a post.
- String of salvaged running lights.
- Immaculate by compound standards.  The canopy isn't fraying.  The grill isn't crusted.  The one space the crew maintains for *themselves*.

This is where the mechanics become *people*.  Rowena runs the bay with authority during shift; up here she's just another person with a drink.  Real talk happens here.  Who's scared.  Whose vehicle came back with claw marks.  Who's thinking about going upwell for good.

If Leight ever gets invited up, it means something.  This is the crew's living room.  You don't get up the ladder without being vouched for.

### The Tub

A welded soaker basin.  One person.  Hot or cold (chiller coil salvaged from a vehicle cooling system).  The luxury isn't hot water (the Cascade has that); it's *cold* water on demand after a shift in the steam, and privacy.

Officially a "decommissioned parts-washing basin they haven't gotten around to breaking down."  The plumbing is "left over from when it was operational."  The chiller coil is "still attached; we'll strip it for parts eventually."  The clean towel on the frame post is harder to explain.

Nobody with authority climbs up to check because nobody wants to antagonize the people who keep the expedition fleet running.  Anyone without authority who finds out understands instinctively: knowing about it means you're trusted, so don't blow it.

### Growth History

1. Rooftop exists because water tanks need access for maintenance.
2. Someone plants a seedling in a salvage tray next to the tanks.
3. Garden grows.  Crew starts eating lunch up there.
4. Someone hauls up a cooking element.  Then seating.  Then the canopy.
5. Now it's *theirs*.  Unspoken but understood.  Not on any map.  No one authorized it.

## Organic Growth (the building over time)

Nobody planned Rowena's bay as it currently exists.  It grew one problem at a time:

1. Stack cans vertically, taller than the largest vehicle.  Park the vehicle beside it.  Stack cans on the other three sides to enclose.  Add service bay doors.
2. Ground floor and one level up become workspaces (electrical, welding, paint).  Upper levels become offices and warehousing.
3. Roof over the courtyard to cover the work area.
4. Second vehicle to work on: extend the pattern.  Three or four service bays.
5. New larger vehicle (Mk2 barge): modify the outermost bays to be taller/wider.  Interior bays can't easily be expanded; they stay for smaller equipment.

The result: a building that reads its own history in its geometry.
