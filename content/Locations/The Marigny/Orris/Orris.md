# Orris

**Type:** Jungle World
**Region:** Outlands ([[Locations/The Marigny/The Marigny|The Marigny]] sector)
**Atmosphere:** Breathable / Ideal
**Life:** Bountiful (forgespawn-infested)
**Orbital Settlement:** [[Locations/The Marigny/Paxton Orbital Station/Paxton Orbital Station|Paxton Orbital Station]]

## Summary

From orbit: a tropical paradise.  Lush, green, breathable, beautiful.  All sensors confirm it.

From the ground: a forgespawn-infested jungle concentrated around an active precursor vault extraction operation.  The sole human foothold is [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon Terminus|Celadon Terminus]], maintained at enormous cost.

## The True History (not public knowledge in full)

### The Vault

A precursor vault exists somewhere on or beneath the surface of Orris.  Long ago, Remnants guarded it.  The forgespawn won that war.  The Remnants are dead (their biomechanical remains may be part of what's being scavenged now).  The forgespawn dispersed across the planet afterward; with no Remnant activity to draw them, they became dormant or scattered thinly through the jungle.  For centuries, Orris was a quiet, beautiful, deadly-only-in-the-normal-ways jungle world.

### The Resort Attempt

A Faction (TBD; one of the Five Founder Clans?) purchased orbital and surface rights.  From-orbit surveys showed paradise.  The plan: exclusive luxury retreat.

What got built:
- Celadon Terminus: cruise-terminal welcome center.  Grand Atrium.  Terrazzo plaza.  Guest-arrival architecture.
- The plaza area: thermobaric-cleared and paved as the resort's main square.  (They were paving it anyway.)
- Partial resort structures beyond the plaza (day spa, at minimum).

The early construction phase went fine.  Occasional wildlife encounters; nothing out of the ordinary for a jungle world.

### The Breach

Someone cracked the vault.  Digging foundations, clearing land, drilling; the exact circumstances are unclear.  They found something valuable inside.  Precursor tech, materials, data; nature TBD.

The breach rang a dinner bell.  Forgespawn across the planet, dormant since the Remnants went silent, suddenly sensed Remnant material being disturbed.  Activity concentrated around the compound.  Escalated fast.

### The Pivot

Construction crews started disappearing.  Then security teams.  By the time anyone connected "we opened something" to "the jungle is trying to kill us," the operation was already pivoting.  Whatever they found in the vault was too valuable to walk away from.  Resort → extraction operation.

### The Present Siege

The compound exists in a tense equilibrium.  Perimeter crews kill forgespawn to maintain the cleared zone; each kill draws replacements inward on a delay.  The system is survivable at steady state.  But the Cartel's narcotic hunting operation is killing more than necessary, tipping the balance and gradually increasing pressure.  If the operation ceased entirely, the forgespawn would eventually disperse back to natural density over decades; but they'd still be here.  They won this planet long ago.  Stopping doesn't make Orris safe; it just makes it quieter.  And nobody's walking away from money in the ground.

## The Jungle

**Flora ("the vines"):** Growth is relentless, especially at night.  Daytime: photosynthesis.  Nighttime: surge growth.  The accelerated vine expansion is likely forgespawn-influenced flora responding to whatever the breached vault is leaking.  Regular jungle was manageable; the forgespawn-touched growth is what makes it a 24/7 war.

The vines deliver a bioelectric shock when severed.  Cut one, your arm goes numb.  Cut a hundred in a shift, you're done for the day.  This is why machete crews rotate frequently and why fire is the preferred tool at night; you don't have to touch the stuff.  Insulated tools are standard issue but wear out fast.

The vine mass exhibits herder behavior; it pushes smaller creatures and debris ahead of its advance front.  It's not growing blindly.  The smell of ozone and rot is the early warning that a dense section is approaching.

**Fauna ("the cats"):** The apex forgespawn predator on Orris.  Feline body shape, large (vehicle-sized), with too many eyes.  Not insect-like clusters; eyes where eyes shouldn't be.  Along the jaw, behind the ears, across the shoulders.  It can watch in every direction without turning its head.

Behavior: pure camouflager.  It goes perfectly still in the canopy, long limbs folded into the branch structure.  It doesn't need to move because it can see everything.  Sensor-invisible.  Physically hard to spot because it simply doesn't move.

Attack: an exhaled hallucinogen/sedative compound.  It breathes on you from above.  You get drowsy, or the jungle starts looking wrong, or you stop noticing danger.  You wander.  You stop cutting.  You walk toward the canopy thinking you see something familiar.  Your crew watches you leave and by the time they realize something's wrong, you're past the perimeter.  The cat doesn't even have to climb down.

**The chum:** When a forgespawn dies, others come.  Whether this is intelligent investigation (checking on their own), territorial behavior (noticing adjacent territory is undefended and moving in), or something else is unclear.  The effect is consistent: every kill creates a territorial vacuum that draws replacements inward from deeper jungle.  Each perimeter kill makes the immediate area safer for a few days and the long-term density worse.

This creates a tense equilibrium.  The crew kills enough to maintain the perimeter; each kill draws a replacement on a delay; the situation hovers at survivable stasis.  The Cartel's hunting operation disrupts this balance by killing more than necessary for defense, pulling in extras from deeper territory and gradually increasing pressure on the compound.

**Sensory concealment:** The forgespawn on Orris are invisible to sensors.  Not physically invisible; you can see them with your eyes if you're looking at the right spot.  But scanners, motion detectors, thermal imaging; all of it slides past them.  This is why orbital surveys showed paradise.  The forgespawn were there the whole time; the instruments just couldn't register them.  Ground crews initially dismissed encounters as "of course there are predators, look at the place" because nothing showed up on their equipment.  By the time the pattern was obvious, it was too late.

This also means perimeter defense is low-tech by necessity.  Sensors are useless against the primary threat.  You need eyes.  Human eyes.  Which is why the crews rotate and burn out.

**The connection to Leight:** His Shade gift makes *things* unnoticeable to people.  The cats make *themselves* unnoticeable to sensors AND use a chemical attack that makes people stop noticing everything.  Same energy, weaponized in the opposite direction.  [[Characters/Leight/Leight|Leight]] has never had reason to test whether his gift works on sensors specifically (he's always operated around people, not machines).  If it does, that's a significant upgrade he doesn't know about.

## The Cartel's Role

[[The Cartel]] does NOT own Orris.  They are the Teamsters: logistics, transport, labor management, supply lines.  They keep the shuttle running, the workers in line, the operation flowing.  They take a massive cut.  Their power is being indispensable infrastructure, not holding the deed.  On top of the legitimate logistics, they run every traditional organized crime racket that makes money, legal or not.

**The narcotic operation (not public knowledge):** The cats' hallucinogenic compound can be refined into a street narcotic.  The Cartel skims biological material off perimeter kills (or runs their own hunting operations) and ships it up the well mixed in with legitimate cargo.  This actively makes things worse: every extra kill creates another territorial vacuum, drawing replacements inward from deeper jungle.  The Cartel is gradually tightening the noose around the compound for profit.  The Faction notices the situation slowly deteriorating but can't explain why.  The Cartel can, and doesn't care.

## Logistics

- **Up (mass driver):** Electromagnetic launch rail.  Unmanned cargo pods to orbit.  Thumps every few hours.
- **Down (retro-rocket drops):** Supply containers with retro-rockets that fire before landing, incinerating the immediate zone.  Workers rush to unload before regrowth.  Containers get repurposed as infrastructure (housing, storage, perimeter material).  Nothing wasted.
- **Off-target drops:** They happen.  Occasionally retrieved depending on how far out they landed and what was on them; a close miss with critical supplies might warrant a recovery sortie.  But anything deep in the jungle is a write-off.  The deep canopy is littered with lost containers slowly being consumed.  Salvage hook for the brave or desperate.
- **Personnel shuttle:** Paxton ↔ Celadon.  ~1 hour transit, every ~2 hours.  For bodies only.

## Defense Rhythm

- **Daytime:** Machete crews on the perimeter.  Maintenance cutting.  Mass driver launches.  Expedition prep.
- **Nighttime:** Flamethrower shifts.  The vine surge accelerates after dark.  The perimeter glows with a ring of fire.  Night is never truly dark.  You go to sleep smelling smoke.
- **Supply drops:** Targeted where growth is worst.  Retro-burn clears the zone; the clock starts immediately.

## The Abandoned Resort

Just past the current perimeter edge: the remains of partially-built resort structures.  Cracked tile floors split by root systems.  A pool filled with something that isn't water anymore.  The skeleton of floor-to-ceiling windows.  Perimeter crews have seen it.  Landmark: "don't push past the spa."

**The Watchtower:** Visible above the canopy from inside the compound: an unfinished vista tower.  Intended as a tourist lookout; a place for resort guests to ascend above the jungle canopy and take in the view.  Never completed, but the structure got tall enough to clear the treeline before construction stopped.  It's unreachable now (well inside forgespawn territory), but it's *there*, visible every day, a reminder of what this place was supposed to be.

It's become a morbid joke among the crews.  "How was night shift?" "Oh, you know... the watchtower's still standing."  Meaning: nothing's changed, we're still here, the jungle hasn't swallowed it yet.  The day it disappears behind the canopy, things have gotten measurably worse.

Potential future use: the upper floors above the canopy might serve as emergency refuge.  The forgespawn are jungle creatures; above the canopy might be above their territory.  Nobody's tested this theory and nobody's eager to.

These ruins are evidence of the timeline (it was safe enough to build here once) but nobody connects the dots publicly.

## Hooks and Open Questions

- **The vault:** Does the Faction even know they breached one?  Where is it?  How deep?  Or is the "vault" theory wrong and the chum is purely death-based?
- **The chum:** Forgespawn death draws others inward (territorial vacuum or investigation).  The Cartel's over-hunting is tipping the equilibrium.  Does anyone on the ground see the pattern?
- **The resource:** What's being pulled out?  Remnant biomechanical remains?  Precursor tech?  Raw materials?  Energy source?
- **Which Faction owns the claim?**  One of the Five Founder Clans?
- **The narcotic:** The Cartel is refining the cats' hallucinogen into a street drug and shipping it offworld.  Who buys it?  How widespread is it?  Does Leight encounter it on Paxton?
- **[[Locations/The Marigny/Paxton Orbital Station/Far Provenance|Far Provenance]] / Ike Sutton:** A precursor-artifact fence on Paxton.  What's being smuggled out of the extraction?  What specific thing is Ike searching for?  And [[Characters/Leight/Leight|Leight]] carries a piece of precursor machinery in his pocket.
- **Shade connection:** [[Characters/Leight/Leight|Leight]]'s power makes things unnoticeable to people.  The cats make themselves unnoticeable to sensors AND chemically suppress awareness in prey.  Parallel mechanisms, different targets.  Is his mutation resonance with the same energy?
- **Leight's sensor potential:** He's never tested whether his gift works on machines.  If it does, that's a significant capability he doesn't know about.
- **Dispersal timeline:** If everything stopped, how long before the forgespawn thin out again?  Decades?
- **Original construction survivors:** Does anyone know the real timeline?  That it used to be safe here?
- **Lost supply drops:** Salvageable containers in forgespawn territory.  Worth the risk?
