## Summary

A sentimental old man whose identity was his craft.  Oren served as a combat engineer and loadmaster on the SH-56C Patroclus cargo variant (universally known as "the Lurch") for decades.  His father served on one.  Possibly his grandfather before that.  The SH-56 is the airframe that won't retire: older than anyone flying it, parts in every junkyard, and every wrench-turner in the sector knows the bolt patterns by heart.

When the *Miravel's Reverie* (née *Hulking Anguish*) came up at auction, Oren bought it as a passion project.  He intended to restore it to the rugged freighter configuration he remembered from his service days.  Stripped the luxury fittings, started reinforcing the frame, rebuilt cargo systems, restored the original military designation.  He was doing it *right*: not junkyard scrap, but proper restoration to a specific iteration of the airframe.  The one his family flew.

He never finished.  Passed before the work was done.  No next of kin claimed him; his property was sold as a lot (land, hangar, and everything in it).  The buyer didn't know or care what the ship was and put it up for resale.

Somewhere aboard, Oren left a meticulous build log documenting everything he did, planned to do, and deliberately left alone.  Nobody's found it yet.

## The Heir

Oren has a living heir (grandson? nephew?) who was off-world in military service when Oren passed, almost certainly serving as an engineer aboard an SH-56, carrying on the family tradition.  By the time military mail caught up with him, the property had already been snapped up by a wealthy buyer who wanted the land.  The "no next of kin" paperwork closed suspiciously fast.  The heir is out there somewhere: quiet, competent, patient, and looking for his grandfather's ship.  He'd recognize the wooden knobs.

## Legacy on the Ship

- Stripped most luxury fittings; restored military designation *Hulking Anguish*
- Reinforced known structural weak points (the strut that always wore prematurely)
- Left the cockpit breach-patch and over-pressurized cabin untouched (recognized them as a soldier's marks)
- Hand-carved wooden knobs on three cockpit switches (family tradition: Grandpa Herschel started it, every Herschel Lurch since has had them)
- A labeled case of hand-machined replacement parts in engineering
- Repurposed the decorative greenwall into a vegetable garden
- Cargo rail system installed two-thirds of the way down the hold (unfinished)
- Disabled some of the PCMS cameras he found (missed others)
