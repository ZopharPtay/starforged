## Summary

Late 30s to mid 40s.  Runs the vehicle repair bay at Celadon Terminus; her name is stenciled on the door in meticulously even letters because she put it there herself and she earned it.  Rockabilly-punk aesthetic in a blue-collar extraction compound: big hair, bandana, coveralls worn open over a fitted top, oversized dark sunglasses when she's outside.  The look is deliberate, curated, and completely incongruous with the mud and rain and sheet metal around her.  "Eccentric" and "elegant" in a context where everyone else looks like they slept in their work clothes, because they did.

Plays the cougar stereotype to the hilt.  Aggressive, theatrical flirtation aimed at anyone new or uncomfortable, delivered with full eye contact and zero shame.  Her crew loves it; they've seen the act a hundred times and still laugh when the new guy goes red.  It's a dominance display disguised as fun.  She controls the social space in her bay the way she controls the workflow: completely, loudly, and on her terms.

Underneath: lonely and stretched thin.  The performance is armor.  She fought for this position and holds it through force of personality and competence in equal measure.  The bay is her queendom; she doesn't want more, she just refuses to let anyone take what she has.

## The Flirtation

She watched men do this badly her entire career: the catcalls, the innuendo, the constant low-grade harassment that passes for banter in mechanical bays across the Forge.  At some point she decided to do it better than them.  Louder, funnier, more precise, more uncomfortable.  She weaponized the thing that was used against her, refined it into an art form, and now deploys it with surgical timing.

Her crew doesn't laugh because they're in on a mean joke.  They laugh because they watched her build this over years, they respect the craft, and she's genuinely funnier than any of them.  The new guy going red is the punchline.  The real joke is that she's their boss and she's better at being a "dude" than the dudes ever were.

She uses it to:
- Establish dominance with strangers immediately
- Keep her crew loyal (shared humor; they're her audience)
- Throw people off-balance before negotiations
- Fill the silence that would otherwise remind her she goes home alone

She's good enough at it that you can't tell if she means it.  That ambiguity is the point.
