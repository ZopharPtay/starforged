## Appearance

Lean, angular, mid-40s.  Moves with a precise economy that says "former manual laborer" but carries himself like someone who stopped taking orders a long time ago.  Clean hands now, but the calluses haven't fully faded.  Dresses simply, nothing flashy.  The wine bottle at the shop is his.

## Personality

**Bold but reclusive.**  Darwin doesn't do small talk and doesn't hang around the shop more than necessary.  When he shows up, it's purposeful: check the books, drop off a client referral, collect his cut.  Makes decisions fast and doesn't second-guess.  Not cruel, but not sentimental either.  Business is business.

Keeps his personal life entirely separate from the shop.  Nobody on Ring 5 seems to know where he lives or what he does with his time.  This is unusual for Paxton, where everyone knows everyone's business in the lower rings.

## Background

Started as a yard laborer on Paxton, probably in his teens.  Worked dock crews, cargo handling, maybe salvage.  Over the years accumulated enough to buy into a few service leases on the lower rings.  Flint's shop isn't his only holding, but it might be the one he pays the most attention to.

The "self-made man who gives ex-cons a second chance" narrative is what he tells people.  It's not entirely false.  But the terms always favor Darwin.

## Relationship with Flint

- Owns the shop lease, the permits, and the client list
- Flint is technically an employee despite the handshake framing of "partnership"
- Takes the majority cut of paying jobs
- Friendly enough in person, not overtly exploitative, but the power dynamic is clear
- His handwriting is the neat, administrative one on the job board: client names, due dates, payment notes
- Visits the shop irregularly; doesn't keep a schedule Flint can predict

## The Mystery (Goal: Solve a mystery)

Darwin is looking into something.  What, we don't know yet.  Possibilities to oracle later:
- Something he found in a ship that came through the shop?
- A disappearance on-station?
- Something from his yard-labor days that never added up?
- Connected to why he's reclusive (hiding from something while hunting for answers)?

His interest in running mechanic shops in the low rings, where ships come and go and nobody asks questions, may be *strategic* rather than purely financial.  He's positioned to see what passes through.
