## Card 1: Personality / Who was the captain?
#### Hearts = personal, emotional, relational.  6 = moderate.

### Prompt
  The ship's assigned officer-in-charge (not the VIP, the pilot/steward): Someone who cared about this vessel.  A mid-career fleet lieutenant who took genuine pride in keeping it spotless and running smooth.  Not ambitious enough for command track, not cynical enough to half-ass a "chauffeur" posting.

### Interpretation
  [[Lt. Maren (Threshold) Kade|Lt. Maren Kade, callsign "Threshold."]]  A hotshot combat pilot with too much pride and ego, reassigned after a rapid depressurization event shredded her lung capacity.  She resented the posting at first because it wasn't the combat role she'd built her identity around, but grew into it.  Her evasion skills came in handy at least once before the end.

## Card 2: Mark left behind (modification/addition)
### Clubs = labor, craft, ingenuity.  4 = subtle/minor.

### Prompt
  A small, practical, hand-crafted touch.  This wasn't a big refit; it was a quiet personal improvement.

### Interpretation
  Threshold's injuries included restricted lung capacity.  As an accommodation, the flight deck is slightly over-pressurized: enough that your ears pop when the doors seal, but not enough to be painful.  This requires the flight deck to have an airlock between it and the rest of the ship, making the layout unusual.  The increased pressure does not carry over to the passenger or cargo areas; it was limited to the pilot's working and living spaces and would not have been allowed to inconvenience the VIP(s).  Nobody has since figured out exactly why it's configured this way, nor had the impetus to disable it.

[[Commander Lantern|Commander "Lantern"]] (Wraiths intelligence officer) was abducted, held for ransom, and eventually executed when the Wardens refused to pay.  The boarders knew they couldn't breach the internal airlock, so they breached the cockpit bulkhead instead, spacing Threshold and rendering the vessel inoperable in one move.

# Narrative

The _Hulking Anguish_ rolled off the line as a [[Stellar Wardens]] fleet dispatch/VIP courier, a versatile mid-size hull used to shuttle high-ranking officers and classified cargo between capital ships and installations.  Its first captain was [[Lt. Maren (Threshold) Kade|Lt. Maren Kade, callsign "Threshold,"]] a hotshot combat pilot reassigned to the role after a rapid depressurization event shredded her lung capacity.  Threshold had too much pride and ego for the posting; she'd earned her name running white-knuckle extractions through narrow gate windows during the Clan wars, and resented being put out to pasture as a glorified chauffeur.  But she grew into it, took quiet pride in keeping the ship razor-sharp, and her combat evasion instincts proved their worth at least once.

Her one lasting modification: the flight deck runs at slightly elevated atmospheric pressure to compensate for her damaged lungs, sealed behind its own internal airlock.  This makes the cockpit a self-contained compartment, separate from the passenger and cargo spaces.  Nobody has since figured out exactly why the pressure differential exists, and nobody has bothered to undo it.

The posting ended violently.  Boarders hit the ship while [[Commander Lantern|Commander "Lantern,"]] a Wraiths of the Elder Imperium intelligence officer, was in transit.  Unable to breach the internal airlock, they cut through the cockpit bulkhead instead, spacing Threshold and disabling the vessel in one move.  Lantern was abducted, ransomed, and eventually executed when the Wardens refused to pay.  In the chaos of the boarding, Lantern stashed a data chip in the false panel compartment concealed in the cargo hold ramp: the Wraiths' asset network, names and covers for operatives embedded across the Forge.  It was never recovered; the Wraiths assumed it was lost with their officer.

The ship was decommissioned, patched, surplus-tagged, and auctioned cheap.  Nobody wanted the bad-luck boat where a pilot died and a spook was lost.  It also left military service with an undocumented false panel concealed in the cargo hold ramp, a standard classified-courier feature disguised as a maintenance access panel that simply never made it into the civilian paperwork.
