## Play

There were no sleds to rent at the arrival pad.

It is a _very_ long walk back to Flint's.  About a quarter of the way through, Leight jokingly decides the tote shall be named The Albatross.  He remembered some old tale about those things weighing you down or something.  It's late and he's making dumb jokes to himself to help the time pass.  The breaks are coming more frequently and taking longer.  The old jin finds its way into his fingers every time he stops.  He sees a private sled painted in the matte brown livery of one of the courier companies momentarily left outside a shop while making a delivery and has to talk himself out of "borrowing" it.

It must have been two hours since the shuttle landed.  The station's corridor lights have started fading to their evening hue, and the general flow of traffic has a noticeable tendency to be heading in the opposite direction as the drift who work here head home toward the more residential areas.

He stops outside an electronics shop, one leg propped up on the tote like a conquering hero as he pretends to check out the display while he catches his breath.  Twenty minutes later The Albatross insists that a poster on a bare stretch of wall simply shouldn't be ignored.  He stops a half hour later and sits on the crate while he pretends to fish some offending debris out of his boot.  

He rounds the last corner.  He's almost free of The Albatross, and Flint's door is in sight.  Barely in sight.  Alright, the last little optimistic voice inside swears it can see the door, at least.  

After another push he sets the crate down beside one of the roving nood shacks, a large area rug and a few stools claiming this particular area as its daily domain.  He actually asks The Albatross if it's hungry while reading over the menu.  If the noodmonger even noticed, he's certainly seen stranger.

Leight sobers up real quick as he's about to pick the crate up for the last stretch to the door.  A wiry middle-aged man who had passed while he was reading the menu walked into Flint's shop.  Must be nice, walking in without thirty kilos of someone else's problems.  

One does not deliver shy products with strangers present, Leight sagely tells himself.  He decides that perhaps the nutripack from earlier has run its course.  He's clearly exhausted and a little stim-burnt.  He re-reads the menu, actually paying attention this time, looking for the cheapest item on it.  Flint can owe him this one.

These roving noods don't stay in business if they aren't good, and these... these are some _very_ good noodles.  Or maybe he's just starving.  Probably both.  

About halfway through his bowl he spots tall, dark, and sinister leaving, heading the same direction he went in.  His courier instincts note that the old man didn't do the "rookie lookie".  Looking both ways as one leaves a clandestine meet is always a dead giveaway that one is new to the game.  This man strolled out like he had every right to be there and he clearly didn't expect anyone to question it.

He finishes his dinner while keeping an eye out for the old man and on the door to see if anyone else is coming or going and notices a little wryly that after eating he doesn't feel quite as inclined to ask The Albatross what it thinks about the situation.   He clearly needed that more than he realized.

Alright, the overhead lights have continued their evening shift and he has dawdled enough to be confident that nobody else is coming out.

His hand term chirps as he is finishing up.  He has a few missed messages from the day, and one from Flint just now: "How much longer?  I'm not waiting all night".

Guess that meeting didn't go so well...  

"Few minutes.  Didn't want to interrupt your date," Leight shoots back.  He purges the message, bringing him back to the queue.  He'll never understand how half these accounts even get his info, but one catches his eye before he swipes to purge all.  "Buy yourself a little something."  A new account pops open with a few credits.  He's not sure if he should be smiling or groaning, but he does both in about equal measure.  Looks like dinner is on Rowena, after all.  He just has to pay from an account that will display his name as "Wet Kitten"... but this guy just saw him asking a box what it wanted for dinner, so he's not exactly winning the Stable Customer of the Day award tonight.

He brings his bowl back to the cart, taps to pay, ("Have a good evening, Mister.... ... Kitty?") and squats down to carry The Albatross the last stretch. 

Leight stumbles into Flint's place tired, sore, and thoroughly dejected.  The Albatross ends its final flight with a bit more of a _thump_ on the counter than he really intended.  He pats the tote by way of apology as Flint looks up.

"Took you long enough.  Bring the sled in so I can lock up for the night, it's been a long day."

Leight doesn't have a reply handy for that.  Rowena's sled?  Was that _not_ her sled?  Did Marrow mess with the new guy?

"The company sled?  The locker bank at the arrival pad... tap your... did you _carry that thing_?   Leight, you...  really?  You drifthead..."  Flint hits a button on the reception desk and the front door's security bars slide home.  "Let's get this unpacked and get you a drink."

Flint carries the tote back to one of the workbenches with what Leight felt was a thoroughly unnecessary amount of theatrical effort.  They pull the greasy parts out, inspecting each in turn and instructing Leight to place them on various workspaces around the shop, presumably depending on the work and tools they required.  He has a quick peek under the dirty cloth before sliding the entire tote into a secure locker, the remaining contents unmentioned.

Flint drops a few credits on the table "for the sore back" before making one last pass through the shop, putting away a few stray items, turning off lights, and checking security details.  "Go wash your face while I finish up here, and then let's get you that drink."
