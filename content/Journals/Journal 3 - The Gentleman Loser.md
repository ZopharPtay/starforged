## Recap

Leight has spent the afternoon wandering Paxton Station, killing time before his 18:00 meeting with [[Flint]] at the Gentleman Loser.  The Hulking Anguish is parked in Flint's service cradle.  He's broke, free, and about to buy a drink he can't afford for a mechanic he needs more than he'd like to admit.

### Location

[[The Gentleman Loser]] bar on [[Paxton Orbital Station]] in  [[The Marigny]] sector.

## Prompt

18:00 approaches.  Leight's plan: arrive slightly late so Flint is already seated.  Go to the bar.  Order Flint a beer, himself a Well Water.  Hope Flint doesn't notice or doesn't call him on it.  Then talk.  He needs work and he needs his e-drive looked at.  Flint is overworked, and any unbilled labor needs [[Zari Darwin]]'s sign-off.

Possible approaches:
- Play it casual and let Flint lead the conversation
- Be direct about needing work; lean on their shared history
- Feel out what Flint's situation actually is before asking for anything

---
## Play

Leight decides to try playing the "fashionably late" card, hoping to skip at least the first round of drinks he really shouldn't afford.  But he _does_ need Flint, so he can't afford to keep them waiting too long either.  He waltzes in around ten minutes late and looks around for his contact on his way to the bar.  Flint must be annoyed by the wait; he broke the unwritten rule and looked up and made eye contact when Leight walked in.  Not an auspicious start.

He points to the bar, despite clearly making his way there, hoping to mollify Flint by offering a drink before they can rip into him for the wait.   He spends a second trying to remember Flint's drink before thankfully remembering this is the Loser.  You can order the tap beer or the same tap beer in a different mug.   He orders a tall beer for Flint, and grudgingly orders himself a Well Water; an old joke order of water with a splash of color usually reserved for kids wanting to look cool or the desperate who want to fit in but can't afford to drink.   It's pretty clear where Leight falls on that spectrum.  Hopefully splurging on the long pour will smooth things over a little.

He sets the mug down in front of Flint and slides into the booth across from them with way more confidence and levity than he's feeling.

"Sorry, friend.  I got caught up wandering the pods and reminiscing.  Hope I didn't miss anything important around here?"

"Just about did," Flint replies after waiting long enough for Leight's grin to start to fade.  "In fact, if you had cheaped on the drink, you might be the one drinking it.  What are you doing here, man?  What."  They take a swig of the draft.  "Why are you asking about me?"  

There were only so many ways to play this, and Flint asked right out.  He wasn't going to try to weasel out of this one without looking like _more_ of a prick.

"I have work."  He almost stopped there but added: "I need work."  It hurt a bit to finally admit it out loud and he didn't bother hiding the flinch of shame.  He did take a long pull of his drink though.  Even in this poor lighting he realized he wasn't fooling anyone; it clearly wasn't beer.  Why did he bother.  "I'm hoping we can work something out.  I'm not looking for charity, but I can't pay up front either."[^compel]

Flint searched the head of their beer like an old mystic reading tea leaves before finishing it.  "How much work are we talking?"  

Leight wasn't entirely sure which direction that was meant; how much work does the ship need, how much work was Leight looking to do, or how much work is there for Flint in the future?  In the end it's pretty much all the same: "I don't know.  It's going to be a bit to get it reliable, then a bit more to get it cleaned up beyond just making it spaceworthy."  Something in Flint's tone lifted a huge weight off Leight's shoulders.  They didn't say yes, but that was definitely not a no.  He hadn't realized how nervous he had gotten.  

"I haven't had a Well Water since I was a kid man, that's just nasty.  Let me get you an actual drink, then we'll talk this out."  Flint gestures to the bartender on their way over and the drinks are poured and waiting when they get there.  Leight noted that _he_ had needed to show cred before the glasses would even hit the bar.

Back at the booth, they pass a short over and nod, as if finally deciding.  "I can't promise anything, and if we're working on barter then I can't commit to timelines, but I think we can help each other.  You're working this off on my schedule, not yours."  They hold their glass out, offering to seal the deal - another long pour for themselves, Leight couldn't help but notice - and Leight clinks his own against it.  It's not like he had a whole lot of options.  The terms were a bit vague for his liking, but work for work was a fair deal, and paid work has to come first in a shop.  It was hard to complain.

They nursed those drinks and talked shop until the overhead fiber-optics shifted to evening hues and the atmo fans kicked down to their lower settings.  Leight couldn't remember the last time he'd had a conversation that wasn't transactional.  This one was, technically, but it didn't feel like it.  It felt like planning a jailbreak.  It felt like planning for the future that might actually happen, for a change, instead of empty dreaming.
