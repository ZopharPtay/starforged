## Recap

[[Characters/Leight/Leight|Leight]] just walked through the front doors of [[Celadon Terminus]] for the first time.  Every previous trip to [[Orris]] ended at the locker bank; Cartel dead drops and a caf while he waited for the shuttle back.  Now he's stepping outside with [[Flint]]'s late thruster assemblies on a sled, looking for a client he's never met to collect a disputed balance.  Whatever he scrapes together determines whether he can cover the parts pickup Flint underfunded.

The next shuttle back to [[Locations/The Marigny/Paxton Orbital Station/Paxton Orbital Station|Paxton]] runs in about two hours.  Miss it and he's stuck planetside overnight.

### Location

[[Orris]], surface.  Just outside [[Celadon Terminus]], the sole human foothold on the jungle world below [[Locations/The Marigny/Paxton Orbital Station/Paxton Orbital Station|Paxton Orbital Station]].  [[Locations/The Marigny/The Marigny|The Marigny]] sector, Outlands.

## Prompt

The doors open.  What hits Leight first: the heat, the smell, the sound, or the sight?

Orris is a jungle world; breathable atmosphere, bountiful life, forgespawn in the deep green.  Celadon Terminus was originally a luxury cruise welcome center; the surrounding area is stacked sea-can worker housing, machete crews maintaining the perimeter by day, flamethrower shifts by night.  But what does it *feel* like to step into it?

The delivery comes first.  Late thrusters, a client who'll push back on price.  Then a pickup Leight can't fully fund.

## Play

The doors are heavier than they look, a leftover from the resort days with real hinges, not pressure seals.  He backs through, dragging the sled behind him, and the air hits him like a wet towel across the face.

He knew it rained here.  He'd heard it through the walls a hundred times, the white-noise battering the Terminus roof while he killed time at the cafshop waiting for return shuttles.  But knowing a thing and standing in it are two different things.  The rain isn't cold or dramatic.  It's warm and constant, like it has always been here and never plans to stop.  His jacket is soaked through inside of three breaths.

The smell is what gets him.  Wet earth and green things growing so hard they're rotting at the same time.  Under that, a sweetness he can't place.  Nothing on the station smells like this.  Nothing *alive* smells like this.

Somewhere off to his left, past the stacked sea-cans, the covered walkways, and the grey-green wall of canopy pressing in at the crater rim, there's a sound.  Low and steady under the rain.  The falls.  He's seen them through windows on the upper concourse, the white thread cutting through green on days the mist cleared enough to bother looking.  He can't see them now.  Just hear them, a bass note underneath everything else.

He stands there a second too long.  Rainwater is already pooling in the creases of the sled cover.  The compound stretches out ahead of him: plascrete paths wet and dark, covered catwalks connecting blocks of housing stacked three high, a few figures moving between them with the lack of urgency of people who've stopped noticing the rain.  Nobody looks up.  Nobody looks at him.

He thumbs the water off his terminal screen and checks the delivery address one more time.  The "address" field is a bit of a joke: "Rowena, Repair Crew".  In what amounts to a mining outpost with tens of buildings, presumably everyone here knows who "Rowena" is and where "the repair bay" is.  He knows the stacked cans are housing from overhearing workers refer to the "stacked 'racks", and he figures the mechanic bay must be a larger building to fit at least a vehicle or three.  Visibility is pretty tight, but that narrows it down to only a few options within view.   He heads off toward the nearest one hoping for a sign.

The large sheet-metal structure sounds like an instrument played by the incessant rain and Leight sees the long trestle tables and bench seating of a mess hall through the door when a worker slips inside.  Someone spray-painted "The Cascade by Fontaine" in large dripping letters above the door; the rain is already wearing it off.  Isn't Fontaine that obnoxious celebrity chef on the streams?  He keeps on down the main strip - pretty much the only strip - until he hits it.  A large double-hangar with over-sized rolling bay doors doesn't really need a sign.  ROWENA is stenciled meticulously above the door anyway.

The bay is warm and loud.  Half a dozen people working on what looks like an expedition crawler, panels open, parts spread on tarps.  The air smells like ozone and cutting fluid and hot metal.  Nobody looks up when he walks in.  He stands there dripping for a second, scanning for someone in charge, before one of the crew notices him.  Big guy, arms deep in a wiring harness.  Doesn't say anything, just tips his chin toward the far end of the bay where a pair of legs in dark, form-fitting coveralls extends from under the belly of something heavy and tracked.

The legs go on for days.  Leight is human and soaking wet and having a bad afternoon; he allows himself exactly one unguarded second of appreciation before getting his face together.  He parks the sled, straightens his jacket like it matters at this point, and walks over.

"Hey.  Looking for [[Rowena]]?  Got a delivery from Flint."

The creeper rolls out.  Big hair, bandana, grease on one cheekbone like she put it there on purpose.  She looks up at him from flat on her back and takes her time about standing, wiping her hands on a rag that's dirtier than they are.  Gives him a slow look, head to boots and back.

"Well.  Aren't you just soaked right through."  She lets that sit for exactly long enough.  "Are you Flint's gift to make up for being so late?  I'm not sure you look old enough to be worth the [[Lore/Equipment/Expedition Hover Barge|Bulldog]] being down an extra week."

Oh.  THIS is the game.  Leight can play this.  He's played this his whole life.  He puts on the grin.  The one that works.

"If I'd known the view was this good, I'd have run the whole way."

Something shifts behind her eyes.  Not surprise, exactly.  Recognition.  She's seen this play before; she's seen it a thousand times, from better-looking men with more money.  The smile she gives him is not the smile of someone charmed.  It's the smile of someone who just got permission.

The warning shot comes low and casual.  "Honey, I've eaten boys prettier than you for breakfast.  You sure you want to play in my yard?"

He should stop.  Every instinct honed by years of reading rooms is screaming at him to pivot, to go professional, to talk about invoices.  But the grin is already committed,  his mouth is already open, and the words are already out.

"I've been told I'm an acquired taste."

The bay goes quiet.  Not silent; compressors still running, coolant still cycling.  Everyone is still pretending to work, but every wrench in the place has stopped moving.

Rowena steps in close.  Close enough that he can smell engine grease and something floral underneath it and the heat radiating off her from working under the crawler.  She's almost his height.  She puts one finger on the zipper of his soaked jacket, right at the sternum, and doesn't pull it.  Just holds it.

"Sweetheart.  You walked into MY house.  A week late.  Dripping on MY floor.  And you want to stand here and play cute with me?"  Her voice drops half an octave.  "I will *ruin* you in front of these nice men and you will *thank me for it* and then you'll ask me very politely if I'd like to sign for my thrusters."

Someone behind him snorts.  Then someone else.  Then the whole bay is laughing and Leight can feel the heat crawling up his neck because she is *right there* and she is absolutely not kidding and he has lost this exchange so completely that there isn't a word for it.

She holds the position for one more heartbeat.  Two.  Then she steps back, pats him once on the cheek like he's a good dog, and turns toward the sled.

"Let's see what Flint sent me.  Late and pretty is still late, sugar.  We're going to talk about the bill."

The crew is still chuckling.  Leight stands there, jacket dripping, face hot, trying to remember what year it is.  He lets out a breath.

Right.  The bill.  Business.  He can do business.

She inspects the thrusters with the casual efficiency of someone who's already mentally installing them.  Pulls one out of the crate, turns it, checks the housing, sets it down.  Satisfied.  Doesn't say so.

"Tell Flint I was going to short him ten percent for the late fee."  She glances back at Leight, coat still dripping by her sled.  "But look at you.  Like a wet kitten someone left on my doorstep.  You're too adorable to send away empty handed."  She pulls up her terminal and taps through the transfer without ceremony.  His terminal chirps in his pocket.

He checks it.  Full balance.  Every credit.

"Pleasure doing business," he manages, and it comes out almost steady.

"Mhm."  She's already walking back toward the crawler and looks back to blow him an over-exaggerated kiss.  "Come by any time, sugar.  I'll put you to work."

He grabs the empty sled out of habit and makes for the bay doors.  Almost out.  Almost free.

"Leave it, kitten."  She doesn't even turn around.

He lets go of the handle.  Doesn't argue.  Doesn't look back.

"Hey."  The big guy.  The chin-point.  He's leaning back from the wiring harness with a grin that says he's been saving this.  "I'm free tonight, if you still want the company, baby."

The whole bay goes up howling and whistling.  Someone's banging a wrench on a workbench.  Leight doesn't turn around.  He pushes out into the rain, which suddenly feels a lot cooler on his face, and lets the door close on the laughter behind him.

Full balance.  He checks the terminal again.  Full balance.  Alright.  He can work with this.

--- 

The Cascade is exactly what the outside promised: a sheet-metal barn with pretensions.  Raised slatted flooring so the mud falls through instead of piling up; he can hear it dripping into whatever's underneath.  Plastic chairs, plastic tables, all of it the same industrial grey that wipes clean with a rag.  Overhead strip lighting that buzzes.  A serving line along the back wall with steel trays and insulated tubs that look like they've never been allowed to run empty.  The air is just as warm as outside and smells like reconstituted protein and industrial soap.

A handful of off-shift workers are scattered around, hunched over trays, not talking much.  Nobody looks up.  He's starting to think that's just how it works down here; everyone's too tired or too wet to care about strangers.

He wanders down the line and when one of the workers grabbing his meal looks up, Leight almost makes a joke asking if the guy prefers the yellow protein or the green; the guy glances at Leight's cheek and smirks, reminding Leight of just how outclassed he was just moments ago and, in a rare moment of self-awareness, he keeps quiet.  He can't possibly know, but that smirk knew something.  

Leight quietly grabs a mug of something hot from the self-serve station at the end of the line, doesn't ask what it is, and drops into a chair in the corner with his back to the wall.  The mug warms his hands.  The drink is bitter and vaguely herbal and exactly what he needs right now.

He pulls up the terminal.  Full balance from Rowena, confirmed.  The supplier address from Flint's message is just as vague and unhelpful: "salvage yard".  He's got an hour before the next shuttle.  Plenty of time.  He takes a long drink, exhales, and wills the heat out of his face.

The jin is already in his hand.  He doesn't remember pulling it out of his pocket.  It rolls across his knuckles, disappears, reappears; the familiar weight of it settling his nerves the way the drink can't.  The black iron catches the strip lighting dully as it tumbles.

That woman.  He replays it in his head and can't find the moment where he lost control of the conversation.  Probably because he never had it.  Probably because she was three moves ahead before the creeper even rolled out.

He finishes the drink, pockets the jin, rakes his fingers through his hair in a vain attempt to make it look like anything other than plastered down, and heads back out into the rain.
