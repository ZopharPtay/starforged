## Recap

[[Characters/Leight/Leight|Leight]]'s first day on the job.  [[Flint]] sent him planetside with a tote of late thruster assemblies and enough credits to cover shuttle fare and a parts pickup.  The delivery client owes a balance, but Flint mentioned the thrusters are overdue.  The parts pickup costs more credits than Flint provided; Flint only fronted half.  The gap between what Leight collects and what he needs is supposed to be his problem.

He left [[Characters/The Hulking Anguish/The Hulking Anguish|the Anguish]] docked in Flint's service cradle, grabbed the package, and headed for the commercial shuttle port.  [[Journal 4 - New Work]] ended with him rolling the sled toward the departure terminal with time to spare.

### Location

En route from [[Locations/The Marigny/Paxton Orbital Station/Paxton Orbital Station|Paxton Orbital Station]] to [[Orris]], the jungle world below.  Leight has made this trip before on Cartel business but never left [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/Celadon Terminus|Celadon Terminus]].

## Prompt

The shuttle drops through cloud cover and Leight gets his first proper look at the world below Paxton.  Nothing has been established about this place yet: what the settlement looks like, who the client is, what the supplier deals in.

The delivery comes first.  Late thrusters, a client who'll push back on price.  Then a pickup Leight can't fully fund without what the client owes.

Possible approaches:
- Play it straight: present the invoice, stay professional, and hope professionalism is enough
- Read the client before talking money; adapt the pitch to what they reveal
- Lean on implied connections (Cartel territory; everyone knows whose backyard this is)
- Accept whatever the client gives, then Shade the shortfall at the supplier's end (hide something, misdirect, swap labels)
- Get creative: barter, offer future courier favors, find leverage in what Flint didn't say

The jungle world is a blank canvas.  What does Leight see when the shuttle doors open?

## Play

The public shuttle to [[Locations/The Marigny/Orris/Celadon/Celadon|Celadon]] is nothing new to Leight.  He made the transit more times than he can count when he was running goods for [[The Cartel]].  Each time he wonders if it would be more comfortable stowing away in the cargo hold of the business shuttles.  He whiles away the hour or so before boarding people-watching and fidgeting with his jin, making it disappear and reappear in his hands - in the traditional manner.  When they announce boarding, he sets the piece spinning on its domed side with a snap of his fingers while checking his pockets to make sure he has everything one last time, then stuffs it in his chest pocket; a reassuring weight.

Navigating the sled down to the shuttle's loading hatch is frustrating but eventually he gets it into the cabin, thumbs off the power stud, and has a seat on the crate.  Space is at a premium, those who didn't get one of the few seats settled down where they can on and around the sled.  The heavily recycled air and overworked ox scrubbers tend to make everyone a bit sleepy; many don't bother fighting it and nap through the transit.  Those still awake attempt courtesy and mumble unfelt apologies when they are jostled any time the thrusters make a course adjustment.

Transit time is a bit over an hour, but it always feels longer.  Leight has heard folks who take the nicer shuttles complain that the transit time isn't long enough to "do anything", but he'd be damned if he could think of anything worth doing on a shuttle like this.  The jolt of breaking atmo catches him by surprise; he must have dozed off, himself.  The ominous creaking of the shuttle's bulk settling on the landing struts on a gravity landing pretty much wakes up anyone else left asleep.  By the time he's able to actually move the sled, he'll be one of the last off; he doesn't bother making any real efforts to move until the majority of other passengers have cleared out.

Another slow walk down an old familiar corridor from the shuttle to the Grand Atrium; from the near silence of the unloaded shuttle ticking as it cools and settles, toward the cacophony of People massed in the main dome.  Giant display panels directing passengers to their pads, speakers announcing current status reports and occasional news updates.  He drifts past the massive bank of lockers out of habit; The Cartel keeps a few of them for dead drops.  He is a little surprised to realize as he passes the cafshop just beyond the lockers that this is officially as far as he's been from the pad on Orris.  He can't remember how many times he's made or retrieved a drop from those lockers and killed time for the trip back drinking a caf, but has he ever actually been out the Terminus doors?  Surely he must have at _some_ point.  "And don't call me Shirley," [[Warden]]'s voice quips in his head.  There had been plenty of runs down and back that needed an extra pair of eyes.

The front doors.  A bit fancy for doors, but considering this is one of the only places to land in this area and Those With Money pass through here along with the drift like him, it's not surprising that they got a slight upgrade.  The whole entryway has the feel of something which used to be Theirs that We've taken over and worn down through the sheer volume of work boots.  There used to be separate entrances for the lower-tier pads, but over the years the fancier and larger vessels got their own expansion and they threw their scraps down the social ladder.

He realizes he's procrastinating and heads to the doors for the first time, slipping his jin back into the pocket it always seems to find its way out of.
